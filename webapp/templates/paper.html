What Can Be Learned from a Daily-Aggregate Marketing Campaign Dataset?

Validity Diagnostics, Overdispersion, and Limits of Causal Interpretation — Nguyen Luong Hai Dang, Duong Quoc Huu, Nguyen Thi Thanh Tien, Nguyen Hoang Linh (Faculty of Artificial Intelligence, FPT University).

Abstract

Public A/B testing datasets frequently omit the experimental-design metadata on which causal interpretation depends. This study re-analyses a daily-aggregate e-commerce dataset commonly framed as a checkout A/B test but structurally resembling a comparison of two marketing campaigns: it lacks the randomisation unit, intended allocation ratio, user identifiers, and session boundaries, making naive causal readings unsafe. To delineate what the evidence can support, we combine an aggregate conversion-rate comparison with sample-ratio-mismatch diagnostics, A/A simulation, overdispersion modelling via a quasi-binomial generalized linear model, and funnel-stage decomposition. The click-pooled comparison suggests that the Test variant converts worse than Control, yet this conclusion loses statistical support once days are treated as the effective unit of variation: daily variance exceeds the binomial benchmark by roughly 163×, an inflation that also drives an extreme false-positive rate in A/A simulations. Funnel decomposition shows the Test variant gaining clicks but losing downstream progression, a pattern more plausibly explained by compositional confounding than by mid-funnel checkout friction. The contribution is diagnostic: daily aggregates can expose fragility and generate hypotheses, but without unit-level tracking they cannot sustain confident causal rollout decisions.

Reproducibility

Fully reproducible pipeline with a single command python main.py (seed 42). Code available on GitHub; dataset publicly available on Kaggle.

Key Contributions

1 · The unit-of-analysis paradox, quantified. Same data: pooled Z-test yields p < 10⁻³⁰, quasi-binomial GLM yields p = 0.342. The difference is explained by dispersion scale 162.95 — not a statistical trick, but a real structural property of the data.

2 · SRM and A/A as structural diagnostics. SRM χ² = 1646.44 and A/A false-positive rate of 87.8% prove that daily aggregate data cannot be read as independent-trials tests — a warning for all analyses on similar public datasets.

3 · The claim-boundary framework. A table mapping "missing metadata → claim limits" helps analysts self-regulate the strength of their assertions, turning a weak dataset into a valuable methodological case study.
