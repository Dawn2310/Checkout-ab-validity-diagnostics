1 · Sample Ratio Mismatch

Under a 50/50 click-level reference allocation, the observed imbalance is virtually impossible by chance (χ² = 1646.44, p → 0). However, since the assignment unit is undocumented, this result has two plausible meanings:

  • If randomised before impression: the click imbalance may be caused by the Test campaign's higher CTR (+67%) — SRM is then partly a treatment effect.
  • If randomised at click/session level: this constitutes strong evidence of allocation failure or logging errors.
Lesson: SRM must be evaluated at the correct randomisation unit (impressions in this dataset), not at downstream funnel stages.

2 · A/A Simulation

Randomly split the 30 Control days into two halves, pool clicks/purchases within each half, run a Z-test — repeat 2,000 times. If days were exchangeable replicates, p-values should follow U[0,1] and only ~5% of tests should reject.

In practice: 87.8% of tests reject — over 17× the nominal rate. The root cause is not a flawed formula, but the non-exchangeability of daily aggregates: weekday effects, spend variation, campaign scheduling, and unobserved traffic composition make each day a different "experiment".

Consequence: Naive pooled inference (pooled Z-test) on daily aggregate data is unreliable for causal conclusions.

A/A Simulation p-value Distribution

Under H₀ the distribution should be uniform — in practice it is massively concentrated near 0, showing that days are not exchangeable replicates.
