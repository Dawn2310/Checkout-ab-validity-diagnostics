Funnel Decomposition: A Compositional Puzzle, Not a Direct Mechanism

Test wins big at the top of the funnel (CTR +67%) but loses heavily in the middle (add-to-cart −30%), then wins again at the bottom (purchase-given-cart +48%). The cautious interpretation: Test changes the user composition at each conditional stage.

Relative Difference by Funnel Step (Interactive)

Hover for per-group rates and Holm-corrected p-values. All 5 steps are statistically significant after Holm correction — but "significant" ≠ "causal".

Two Stories That Both Fit the Data

📐 Design-Mechanism Story

The Test landing page attracts attention but introduces friction before cart formation — "pretty but clunky".

🎯 Selection Story (more cautious)

Test (via broader ad targeting) attracts additional low-intent clickers. Mid-funnel rates decline as a compositional consequence of expanding the top of the funnel — even if the checkout mechanics are identical.

Why prefer Story 2? Clicks are a post-treatment subset: when treatment changes the probability of clicking, the clicker population of Test ≠ that of Control. All conditional rates downstream of clicks are subject to post-treatment selection bias.

Implications for Follow-Up Research

  • Randomise at the correct funnel entry point.
  • Retain user/session IDs to separate mechanism effects from compositional effects.
  • Record traffic source to control for compositional confounding.
