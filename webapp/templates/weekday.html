Weekday Patterns: Hypothesis-Generating Only

Sign-reversing effects across days of the week — from −55.4% (Saturday) to +63.0% (Sunday). But each weekday has only 4–5 paired observations, so these are exploratory signals, not deployment rules.

Forest Plot: Absolute CR Difference by Weekday (Interactive)

Square markers = significant after Holm correction; horizontal bars = 95% confidence intervals. Hover for detailed segment statistics.

Why This Cannot Be a Deployment Rule

  • Extremely thin samples: only 4–5 paired days per weekday within the 30-day window.
  • Multiple testing: 7 simultaneous comparisons — Holm-corrected but still exploratory and not pre-registered.
  • Mixed regimes: evidence that the pooled average is blending different "traffic regimes" over time — exactly the overdispersion documented on the Results page.
No conclusion such as: "enable Test on Sundays, disable on Saturdays". Such a claim requires a new experiment: pre-register weekday as a moderator, run for a longer period, preserve the randomisation unit, record traffic-source covariates.

The Real Value of This Signal

The sign-reversal pattern is additional evidence for the paper's central thesis: the pooled average conceals complex temporal structure. It explains why daily variance is ~163× larger than binomial, and reinforces the recommendation that rollout decisions require a properly logged, unit-level experiment.

In the paper, this section is presented as "Weekday patterns are hypothesis-generating only" and uses a forest plot to emphasise uncertainty rather than point estimates.
